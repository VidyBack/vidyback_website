Why Readability Actually Affects Conversion

Shoppers don't read product pages the way they'd read an article, they scan them, deciding in seconds whether to keep reading, add to cart, or bounce, especially on a phone screen. Copy that's easy to skim, short sentences, plain words, gets the key selling points across before that decision happens. Dense, jargon-heavy paragraphs are more likely to be skipped entirely, which means the effort spent writing them never actually reaches the shopper.

What "Good" Readability Looks Like, by Content Type

Product and collection pages generally perform best around a 60-80 Flesch Reading Ease score, conversational, roughly 6th-8th grade level. Blog content can comfortably run a bit denser, since readers arrive already engaged and willing to spend more time. Legal text, privacy policies, terms of service, naturally scores low on any readability formula, and that's expected; it's optimizing for precision, not skimmability, so don't apply the same 60-80 target there.

Before and After: A Quick Example

Before

"Our meticulously engineered ergonomic office chair utilizes premium-grade breathable mesh fabrication in conjunction with an adjustable lumbar support mechanism, thereby facilitating optimal postural alignment throughout extended periods of seated productivity."

Flesch Reading Ease: ~-29 (Very Difficult, graduate level — Flesch scores can go negative on dense enough text)
After

"This ergonomic office chair keeps you comfortable all day. Breathable mesh fabric stays cool. Adjustable lumbar support keeps your back aligned, even on long work days."

Flesch Reading Ease: ~68 (Easy to Fairly Easy, 6th-8th grade)

Same product, same features, dramatically easier to skim, just by shortening sentences and swapping "utilizes premium-grade breathable mesh fabrication" for "breathable mesh fabric."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good Flesch reading score for product descriptions?

Most ecommerce product copy performs best in the 60-80 range, conversational and skimmable. Scores below 50 usually mean the copy has drifted into dense, jargon-heavy writing that's harder to skim on a phone, which is where most shopping happens.

Does readability actually affect sales?

Indirectly, yes. Shoppers scan product pages in seconds rather than reading carefully, especially on mobile, so copy that's easy to skim gets the key selling points across before they bounce. Dense paragraphs full of long sentences and jargon are more likely to be skipped entirely.

Is the syllable count 100% accurate?

No, and no browser-based tool's is without a full pronunciation dictionary. This tool uses a standard vowel-group counting heuristic, which is accurate for the large majority of English words but can be off by one syllable on unusual or irregular words. Treat the score as a strong estimate, not an exact measurement.

What's the difference between Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level?

They're calculated from the same underlying sentence-length and syllable-count data, just expressed differently. Flesch Reading Ease is a 0-100 score where higher means easier to read. Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level converts that into an approximate US school grade level needed to easily understand the text.
