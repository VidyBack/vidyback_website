Why Your Shopify Store Isn't Getting Sales From Instagram (And What to Actually Do About It)

June 12, 2025 6 min read VidyBack Team

You set up the store. You photographed the products. You made the Instagram profile look clean. You even posted a few times with hashtags you found online. And then you checked your sales dashboard and saw... a big fat zero.

If that sounds like you, you're not alone. This is one of the most common things store owners go through, especially in the first 6 to 12 months. And the good news? The problem usually isn't your product.

Let's talk about what's actually going on.

The Real Problem: Posting Isn't the Same as Marketing

There's a big difference between putting content out there and actually getting people to notice it, care about it, and click through to your store. Most new store owners focus almost entirely on the first part, and then wonder why the second part never happens.

Instagram isn't a magical traffic machine. It's a platform where attention is incredibly competitive. You're fighting against influencers, big brands, friends, memes, and pet videos all at once. To win even a small piece of that attention consistently, you need to be showing up in a way that people actually want to stop and look at.

"The stores that grow on Instagram aren't necessarily posting more. They're posting smarter — with content that looks native to the platform and speaks directly to what their buyers care about."

The Three Things That Actually Move the Needle

1. Your Content Doesn't Look Like Content, It Looks Like an Ad

Scroll through your own Instagram feed for a second. What makes you stop? It's usually something that feels real, visually interesting, or tells a quick story. What makes you keep scrolling? Anything that looks like a banner ad.

A lot of store owners post product photos with their logo slapped on them and a price in the caption. That's not content. That's a catalog page. People don't follow stores to be sold at — they follow because the content is entertaining, educational, or aspirational.

The stores doing well are showing the product in use. They're making short videos where someone unboxes it, wears it, uses it in a real context. Even a quick Reel showing how your candle smells different when you pair it with a specific playlist is more engaging than a product photo with a discount code.

2. You're Posting Too Inconsistently

Instagram's algorithm rewards consistency above almost everything else. If you post three times in one week and then disappear for two weeks, the algorithm basically deprioritizes your account. It starts showing your posts to fewer people.

The brutal truth is that you need to be showing up at least 4 to 5 times a week to stay visible. That sounds exhausting, and for someone running a store solo or with a tiny team, it genuinely is. Which is exactly why a lot of store owners give up on it.

3. You're Not Targeting the Right People

Getting 500 followers who have zero interest in what you sell is worth less than 50 followers who genuinely love the niche your products are in. Quality over quantity is real on Instagram.

If you sell handmade leather goods, your content needs to be in the spaces where people who care about craftsmanship, durability, and style already hang out. That means using hashtags they actually follow, collaborating with accounts they already engage with, and speaking the language they use to talk about what they love.

What Actually Helps: A Few Things Worth Trying

  • Post video first. Reels get significantly more reach than static posts right now. Even a simple 15-second product demo beats a product photo every time.
  • Use your captions to connect, not just describe. Instead of "Blue ceramic mug – $24," try "That first coffee in the morning hits different in a mug you actually like. This one's hand-thrown and holds heat for over an hour."
  • Reply to every comment and DM, especially early on. Engagement signals to the algorithm that your content is worth showing to more people.
  • Pin your best posts. When someone visits your profile for the first time, your pinned posts are what they see. Make them count.
  • Post at consistent times. Find when your audience is online (check your Instagram Insights) and stick to that schedule.

The Content Bottleneck Is Real

Here's the part most people don't talk about: knowing what to do and actually doing it consistently are two completely different things. Making good video content takes time. Writing good captions takes energy. Scheduling posts every day takes discipline that most store owners just don't have left over after actually running their business.

The Honest Bottom Line

Getting real traction on Instagram as an ecommerce store takes time, consistency, and content that people actually want to watch. There's no shortcut around those three things. But you can make the process a whole lot less painful.

Start with one change this week. Pick video. Make one 15-second Reel showing your product being used. Don't overthink it. Post it, engage with everyone who comments, and repeat that next week.

Small, consistent effort beats occasional perfect posts every time.
