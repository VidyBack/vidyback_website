Instagram & TikTok Algorithm Changes 2026: What Small Ecommerce Brands Need to Know

August 11, 2026 8 min read VidyBack Team

If your reach suddenly tanked this year and you can't figure out why, you're not imagining it. The Instagram algorithm 2026 update changed how content gets ranked in some pretty significant ways, and TikTok made its own major shift around the same time. If you run a small Shopify store and you've been posting the same way you always have, only to watch your numbers slide, this is probably why.

The good news is that most of these changes actually favor small, original accounts over big content farms and repost pages. Here's what changed, what it means for you, and what to actually do about it this week.

What Changed on Instagram

Likes Matter a Lot Less Now

For years, likes were treated as a rough signal of how much people cared about a post. That's not really true anymore. Instagram algorithm changes for small business accounts in 2026 have quietly downgraded likes as a ranking signal and pushed weight toward saves, shares, and DM conversations instead. A like takes half a second and doesn't tell the algorithm much. Sending a post to a friend, saving it to come back to later, or replying in a thread does.

Practically, this means content built purely to be "likeable" isn't going to travel the way it used to. Content that gives someone a reason to save it (a tip, a size guide, something they'll want to reference) or share it (something funny, something relatable, something they think a friend needs to see) is what actually moves now.

DM Shares Are Doing a Lot of Heavy Lifting

This is closely related, but worth calling out on its own. When someone sends your Reel or post to a friend in DMs, that's one of the strongest signals Instagram has that the content is genuinely good. It's a much higher bar than a double-tap, and the algorithm treats it that way.

If you want to know how the Instagram algorithm works in 2026 in one sentence: it's optimizing harder than ever for content people actually want to talk about with someone else, not just glance at and scroll past.

The Repost Penalty Is Real

Instagram also extended its crackdown on unoriginal content this year. Where the rules previously applied mainly to Reels, they now cover photos and carousels too. Accounts that mostly repost content they didn't create or meaningfully edit are getting cut off from recommendations in the main feed and Discover tab. Watermarked reposts, screenshot carousels, and borrowed infographics with no real transformation all get flagged as unoriginal, even with credit given in the caption.

This one matters a lot if your store's account has leaned on curated or aggregated content to fill the calendar. It's not enough anymore to repost a trending video and slap your logo on it. Instagram wants to see content you actually made or substantially transformed.

What Changed on TikTok

Follower-First Testing

TikTok made a structural change to how new videos get distributed. Instead of immediately testing a fresh upload against a broad, random pool of users, TikTok now shows it to your existing followers first. How that smaller group responds, especially completion rate, rewatches, shares, and saves, determines whether the video gets pushed out to a wider audience at all.

This is a real shift from how TikTok used to work, and it explains a lot of the "why did my views suddenly drop" confusion going around. If your followers aren't engaging quickly and strongly, your video may never make it past that first test pool, no matter how good the content actually is.

Completion Rate Is King

The bar for what counts as a "good" completion rate has gone up too. Videos are expected to hold attention closer to the 70% mark now, a noticeably higher bar than a couple of years ago. Shares and saves are weighted well above likes in TikTok's ranking as well, which lines up with what's happening on Instagram.

Shoppable Content Is Getting Priority

For ecommerce accounts specifically, TikTok has continued leaning into shoppable formats, favoring content that's built to move someone from watching to buying rather than just watching and scrolling on. Product-forward, native-feeling videos are getting more of a fair shot than they used to, especially when they're not relying on recycled trending sounds.

What This Actually Means for Small Ecommerce Accounts

Here's the part that should be encouraging rather than stressful: these changes mostly hurt the accounts that were gaming the old system, not the ones doing honest, original work.

"'Original' and 'made by a real small business' is exactly what both platforms are trying to surface more of right now."

Big repost and aggregator accounts built entirely on other people's content are the ones losing reach on Instagram right now. Large TikTok accounts coasting on follower count alone without holding attention are the ones getting stalled by follower-first testing. Small stores that post original product content, even simple, unpolished stuff, are actually in a decent position, because "original" and "made by a real small business" is exactly what both platforms are trying to surface more of.

You don't need a huge following or a big production budget to do well under these rules. You need to post real content about your actual products, consistently, in a way that gives people a reason to save it, share it, or watch it to the end.

What to Do About It This Week

You don't need to overhaul your entire strategy overnight. A few practical shifts go a long way:

  • Stop reposting, start creating. If a chunk of your content calendar has been repurposed or aggregated content, swap it for original product videos, even simple ones. This protects you from Instagram's repost penalty and gives TikTok's follower-first test something worth engaging with.
  • Design for saves and shares, not just likes. Think "would someone send this to a friend" or "would someone want to find this again later" before you post. A quick styling tip, a size comparison, or an honest before/after does more work than a generic product shot.
  • Hook fast and hold attention. With TikTok's completion rate bar sitting higher and follower-first testing deciding whether your video goes anywhere at all, the first couple of seconds and the overall pacing matter more than ever.
  • Post consistently, not sporadically. Both platforms reward accounts that show up reliably. A steady 3 to 5 posts a week beats an occasional burst followed by silence.
  • Keep a real content pipeline going. This is the part that trips most small teams up, not the strategy but the time it takes to keep producing original video week after week.

FAQ

Why is my Instagram reach dropping in 2026?

A few likely culprits: likes carry much less ranking weight now, so content optimized for likes alone underperforms. If your account posts a lot of reposted or lightly edited content, you may also be caught by the new repost penalty, which limits recommendations for accounts that primarily share unoriginal photos and carousels.

What is TikTok follower-first testing?

It's the process where TikTok shows a new video to your existing followers before deciding whether to push it to a wider audience. If that initial group doesn't watch, share, or save at a strong enough rate, the video may never reach the broader For You Page, regardless of how strong the content is.

What is the Instagram repost penalty in 2026?

It's an update that limits recommendations for accounts whose posts, including photos and carousels, are mostly reposted content that wasn't materially created or edited by the account. Simple additions like a watermark, credit, or caption don't count as original under the new rules.

What's the best content strategy for small Shopify stores in 2026?

Post original product content consistently, aim for a handful of posts per week rather than sporadic bursts, design content around saves and shares rather than likes, and lean on tools that can turn your product catalog into fresh video content without eating up your whole week.

How do I increase reach on Instagram as a small business?

Focus on original content only, since reposted material is actively penalized. Prioritize formats that encourage saves and DM shares, like tips, guides, or relatable moments, over content that's only good for a quick like. Consistency matters too. Posting regularly signals to the algorithm that your account is active and worth recommending.
