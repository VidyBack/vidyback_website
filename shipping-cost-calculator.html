What Is Dimensional Weight, and Why Do Couriers Charge By It?

Dimensional (or volumetric) weight is a courier's way of billing for the space a package takes up, not just how heavy it is. A truck or cargo plane has limited volume, not just a limited weight capacity, so carriers calculate an "equivalent weight" from a box's dimensions and charge whichever number is higher: the actual weight on a scale, or the dimensional weight from its size. This is why a large, lightweight box can cost as much to ship as a small, dense one.

Why "Cheap-Looking" Bulky Items Often Cost More Than Expected

Sellers are frequently caught off guard by this. A product that feels light in your hands, like a lampshade, a pillow, or anything packed with a lot of void fill, can rack up a dimensional weight several times its actual weight, and that's the number your courier bills against.

Worked example: A box measuring 40 × 30 × 20 cm weighs 2 kg on a scale.

Dimensional weight = (40 × 30 × 20) ÷ 5000 = 4.8 kg

Since 4.8 kg > 2 kg, the courier bills you for 4.8 kg, not the 2 kg the package actually weighs.

That gap, 2.8 kg of "invisible" weight, is pure packaging inefficiency. Sizing boxes tighter to the product is often the single cheapest lever a store has to cut shipping costs.

Common DIM Divisors by Carrier Type

The divisor your carrier uses depends on their service and unit system. Always confirm the exact figure in your own rate card, but as a general reference:

DivisorTypically Used ByUnits
5000Most international couriers (DHL, FedEx Intl, UPS Intl)cm / kg
6000Some air freight and express air servicescm / kg
139US domestic ground servicesin / lb
166US domestic air servicesin / lb

A lower divisor means a higher dimensional weight for the same box, so a service using 5000 will always calculate a heavier "equivalent weight" than one using 6000 for identical dimensions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dimensional weight?

Dimensional (volumetric) weight is an estimate of how much space a package takes up relative to its actual weight, calculated as length x width x height divided by a carrier-specific DIM divisor. Couriers bill you for whichever is higher, actual weight or dimensional weight.

How do I reduce my dimensional weight charges?

Use the smallest box that safely fits your product, avoid excess void fill and packaging, and consider poly mailers or slim boxes for lightweight items. Reducing any one dimension lowers the volumetric weight calculation directly.

What DIM divisor should I use?

It depends on your carrier and service. Most international couriers use 5000 for centimeters and kilograms, some air freight services use 6000, and US domestic carriers typically use 139 or 166 for inches and pounds. Check your carrier's rate documentation to confirm.

Why did my courier charge more than my package's actual weight suggested?

Your package was likely billed on dimensional weight rather than actual weight. Bulky, lightweight items like pillows, lampshades or packaging-heavy products often have a dimensional weight well above their actual weight, and carriers always charge the higher of the two.
