Black Friday Social Media Plan for Small Online Stores: A Simple Four-Phase Timeline

October 26, 2026 5 min read VidyBack Team

It's the last week of October and your inbox is already full of "Black Friday prep" emails telling you to plan a six-week campaign, build a landing page, film a hero video and brief an influencer. You have a store, a few hundred products if you're lucky, and about four spare hours a week. So you do what most small store owners do: you tell yourself you'll sort it out closer to the day, and then you post a rushed "30% OFF" graphic on the Wednesday before.

That rushed graphic is the thing worth fixing. A good Black Friday social media strategy for a small online store isn't bigger than a normal month. It's earlier, simpler and more repetitive than most advice suggests.

The Misconception: Black Friday Is One Day

Most small stores treat Black Friday as a single post on a single Friday. Shoppers don't behave that way. A lot of people spend the weeks before it quietly deciding what they're going to buy and waiting for a discount to confirm the decision. If the first time they hear from you is the morning of the sale, you're competing with every other inbox and feed at its loudest.

The stores that do well are usually the ones that were already in the feed in early November, showing the product, saying a sale was coming and making it easy to remember. You don't need a huge audience for this to work. You need your products in front of the people you already have, a few times, before the price drop.

A Simple Four-Phase Timeline

In 2026, Black Friday lands on November 27, with Cyber Monday on November 30. Here's a plan that fits around a normal workload.

Phase 1: Tease (now until about November 13)

Pick the 5 to 10 products you'll actually discount. Not the whole catalog. Post short product videos of those items with no mention of a price at all, and add one line near the end of a few of them: "Something's coming at the end of November." The point is familiarity, not urgency.

Phase 2: Announce (November 14 to 22)

Now say what the offer is. Keep it concrete: "20% off sitewide, Friday to Monday" lands better than "huge savings." Before you post it, do the maths. If a 20% discount wipes out your margin on a product, it shouldn't be in the sale. The free profit margin calculator takes a minute and can save you from an expensive weekend. If you're using a code, the discount code generator gives you something short enough to say out loud in a video.

Phase 3: Sale days (November 27 to 30)

Post once a day on the platforms where you already have followers, and use the same product clips you've been posting, now with the offer on screen. Don't make new content under pressure. Reminding people "ends Monday night" on the last two days does more than any new creative.

Phase 4: Last chance and thank you (December 1 to 3)

Extend nothing unless you really mean to. One post that says the sale has ended and thanks people is enough, and it protects the trust that makes your next sale believable. Then move on to your regular schedule.

What to Post, Specifically

Small stores tend to overcomplicate the content. A short product video per item covers almost everything you need:

  • 5 to 15 seconds, vertical, one product. The product in use or being unboxed, with the name and the price on screen.
  • Gift angle for the top sellers. "Under $30" or "under £25" framing works because many November shoppers are buying for someone else and searching by budget.
  • Shipping cutoffs. If you ship to the US, UK or Canada, say plainly when the last safe order date is for Christmas delivery. People appreciate the honesty, and it prevents a pile of angry December emails.
  • A pinned post or story. One place that always shows the current offer, so a new visitor doesn't have to scroll to find it.

On Instagram, keep captions short and stick to a few relevant hashtags, no more than five. A stack of thirty generic sale tags won't help a post that already tells people what the product is and what it costs.

Don't Rebuild Content for Every Platform

The common trap is making a separate Instagram post, a separate TikTok, a separate Facebook update and a separate Pinterest pin for every product. For ten products across four platforms, that's forty pieces of content in a month when you're also packing orders.

Make one vertical clip per product and post that same clip everywhere it fits. Reels, TikTok, Shorts, Facebook and Pinterest all handle vertical video. Change the caption slightly, and that's enough. If you want a deeper look at this, the post on repurposing one product video across five platforms covers it.

Batch It Now, Schedule It, Stay Out of the Feed

The real risk during Black Friday week isn't a weak strategy. It's that you're busy. Orders come in, a courier delays something, a customer asks about sizing, and the posting slips for three days. The fix is to produce and schedule the whole run in one sitting before the rush starts: tease videos first, announcement posts second, sale-day reminders last, all queued with dates.

A reasonable target is two hours this week to build and schedule everything through the first week of December. After that, your only job on the day is answering messages and shipping.
