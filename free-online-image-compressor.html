    Why Smaller Product Images Matter

    Image weight is usually the biggest reason a product page loads slowly, and speed affects more than your hosting bill. Google uses page experience signals such as Largest Contentful Paint in its rankings, and on most product pages the largest element is the hero product photo. Speed also affects conversion: shoppers on mobile data leave a page that takes more than a few seconds to show the product. A 4 MB photo straight from a camera or phone is one of the slowest things you can put on a listing.

    Compressing before you upload means your store, marketplace listings, emails and ads all start from a lighter file. It also helps with storefront themes and apps that don't optimize images for you.

    Target file size for product images

    Aim for under 200-300 KB per product image. 2048 px on the longest side is plenty for zoomable Shopify product images. Most listing thumbnails display far smaller than that.

    JPG vs PNG vs WebP: which to use

    FormatBest for
    WebPProduct photos. Usually 25-35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, and it supports transparency.
    JPGProduct photos where universal compatibility matters, such as marketplaces, email and older systems.
    PNGLogos, graphics, text overlays and cutouts that need a transparent background. PNG is lossless, so photos saved as PNG get large.

    WebP is the best default for your own storefront. Some marketplaces, feed tools and older platforms still expect JPG or PNG, so check the upload requirements before you convert.

    Removing EXIF data

    Photos from phones and cameras carry hidden EXIF metadata: camera model, capture time, and sometimes the GPS location where the photo was taken. Stripping it saves a few kilobytes per image. It also means you don't publish where you shoot your products, which is often your home.
