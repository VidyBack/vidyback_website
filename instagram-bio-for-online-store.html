Instagram Bio for an Online Store: What to Write and Where to Link

November 20, 2026 6 min read VidyBack Team

Your Instagram feed is finally in decent shape. A few product videos, some clean photos of the new stock, a post most weeks. Then someone taps your name from a Reel, lands on your profile, and leaves a few seconds later without ever visiting the shop. Often the problem isn't the posts. It's the few lines at the top of the profile, written in five minutes when the account was set up.

Your Instagram bio is the one part of your profile that nearly every visitor reads, and for a small online store it has a specific job. This guide walks through what to put in it, line by line, with an example you can adapt.

The misconception: a bio is where you describe your brand

Most store owners treat the bio like an "about us" page. "Passionate about slow living. Handmade with love. Est. 2021." It sounds nice, but it doesn't answer the questions a new visitor actually has: what do you sell, is it for me, and where do I buy it?

A more useful way to think about it: your bio is a signpost, not a mission statement. Someone who found you through a Reel or a hashtag has already seen one product. The bio's job is to tell them what else you sell, give them one reason to trust you, and point them to the shop.

What an Instagram bio for an online store needs to do

Instagram gives you 150 characters for the bio text, plus a name field, a link section and, on business accounts, a category label and contact buttons. That's not much room, so each part should pull its weight. For a store, a useful bio answers four things, in this order:

  1. What you sell, in plain words. "Hand-poured soy candles" beats "Cozy vibes for your space." A stranger should understand your product in a couple of seconds.
  2. Who it's for or what makes it different. One specific detail: "for small apartments," "vegan and cruelty-free," "sizes 4 to 24," "made in small batches in Toronto."
  3. One practical detail that answers a buying question. Shipping works well here: "Free UK shipping over £40" or "Ships in 2 business days." Only include it if it's true right now.
  4. One call to action that points at the link. "Shop new arrivals below" or "Browse the full range." Just one.

Use the name field for words people search

The name field is the bold line above your bio, and it's separate from your @username. It's searchable inside Instagram, which makes it worth using for a short product description, not only your shop name.

For example, instead of "Willow & Wick," try "Willow & Wick | Soy Candles." Someone searching Instagram for soy candles now has a better chance of finding your account. Keep it readable, though: "Willow & Wick | Soy Candles Gifts Home Decor Wax Melts UK" just looks like spam.

A worked example: before and after

Here's what a rewrite can look like for an example ceramics shop that sells through Etsy.

Before: "Clay lover 🌿 Making pretty things in my little studio ✨ DM for custom orders 💌 Link below"

After:

  • Name: Fern Studio | Handmade Mugs
  • Bio line 1: Wheel-thrown stoneware mugs and bowls
  • Bio line 2: Dishwasher safe, made in small batches in Bristol
  • Bio line 3: Ships across the UK in 3 days
  • Bio line 4: New glazes every Friday ⬇️

The "before" version tells you the maker enjoys their work. The "after" version tells a visitor what the products are, removes a practical worry (will it survive the dishwasher?), sets a shipping expectation and gives them a reason to check back. It also fits inside the 150-character limit, line breaks included.

One caution about that last line: only promise a schedule you actually keep. "New glazes every Friday" is a good hook for a shop that really does release on Fridays, and a broken promise for one that doesn't.

Where your bio link should point

At the time of writing, Instagram lets you add several links to your profile, but usually only the first one shows on the profile itself, with the rest behind an extra tap. So treat the first link as the one that matters. A simple decision rule:

  • If most of your posts feature new arrivals, link to your "new in" collection page rather than your homepage.
  • If you're running a seasonal push, such as holiday gifting, link to that collection and switch it back when the season ends.
  • If you sell on Etsy, link to your shop page or the specific shop section your recent posts talk about.
  • If you use a link-in-bio page with several buttons, keep it to a handful. A page with 15 buttons recreates the same confusion you were trying to fix.

Whatever you choose, make the link match your captions. If a Reel says "the green mug is in our bio link" and the link opens a homepage where the green mug is three clicks deep, plenty of people won't bother. Our guide to writing Instagram captions for your products covers how to keep that call to action clear in the post itself.

Let Story Highlights do the rest of the work

Story Highlights sit right under the bio and act like a second, visual menu. For a small store, four or five are usually enough:

  • New in
  • Best sellers
  • Shipping and returns
  • Care tips or how it's made
  • FAQ (sizing, materials, custom orders)

Give them short labels and simple covers in your shop colors so they read like a menu, not a scrapbook. If you're not sure what your colors and fonts should be, our post on keeping your social media branding consistent walks through picking a small set and sticking to it.

Common bio mistakes and how to fix them

  • Hashtags in the bio. Hashtags there are clickable and can send visitors off to other accounts selling similar things. If you use one at all, make it your own branded tag. For hashtags in posts, see how many hashtags to use on Instagram.
  • A dead or outdated link. A sale page that ended two weeks ago, or a collection you deleted. Check the link every time you change your store's collections.
  • Vague claims. "Best quality" and "the perfect gift" don't tell anyone anything. Swap them for a fact: the material, the size range, where it's made.
  • Never updating it. Reread your bio at the start of each season, when your shipping terms change, or when you add a new product category.

A five-minute bio checklist

  1. Could a stranger say what you sell after reading the first line?
  2. Does the name field include one plain product word, not a list?
  3. Is there one practical detail, such as shipping, sizing or materials, that's true today?
  4. Is there exactly one call to action?
  5. Does the first link go to the page your recent posts are about?
  6. Are your Highlights labeled like a menu?
